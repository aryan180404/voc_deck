The loudest feedback was not where we were looking

The first version read public reviews from app stores and social media. When I showed it to a product lead, his answer changed the project:

  • External feedback volume is low.
  • Most feedback arrives internally, through support emails, chat and call-centre tickets.
  • Nobody reads all of it, so a serious problem in one district can sit unnoticed for weeks.
What he asked for

A collator and tracker for tickets and emails that suggests product fixes and creates enough noise to get burning issues in focus. Later: a shared place where product and tech teams add solutions, agree an escalation matrix and mark what is resolved.

Then the useful details are picked out

  • Order number, city, district and time, read from the message itself.
  • Quoted earlier replies and email signatures cut away.
  • Hindi, Hinglish and six other languages kept in their own words, so nothing is lost in translation.

The model labels. Code counts.

Gemini reads each message and picks its problem from a fixed list, its sentiment and its severity. It never produces a number on the dashboard: every count, rate and ranking is computed in code, and every insight has to cite real messages.

  • A wrong answer from the model is rejected and retried, then falls back to keyword rules.
  • Every result is cached, so a rerun costs nothing.

Make the burning issues impossible to miss

Support volume is low, so waiting for big numbers is too slow. A problem becomes a burning issue when any one of these is true:

Critical

Food safety or a double charge. One message is enough.

Clustered

Three or more complaints about the same problem in one district within seven days.

New

A group of similar complaints that fits no existing category, and is mostly recent.

Reopened

Marked resolved, yet three or more complaints arrived afterwards.

Product feedback, kept apart from operations

Feature request

What users want the company to build.

Product friction

What stops users from having a good experience.

Usability issue

What makes existing features hard to use.

Operational issue

Delivery, pricing, payments and logistics.

Emerging opportunity

A rising or particularly compelling suggestion, however few said it.

  • One switch, Product or Operations, filters every page.
  • Emails, tickets and LinkedIn count fully; app stores and social media count less.
  • LinkedIn has its own page, with a link to every post.

One LinkedIn post about hiding prices from guests became Zomato's Host Mode within days. Volume is not the only signal.

It found a problem nobody had named

One problem was hidden in the practice data without a category of its own: delivery partners asking customers for extra cash on orders already paid online.

The tool raised it as a new category, New: extra cash, without being told to look for it.

1

Groups messages with similar wording inside each category.

2

Checks whether a group is much newer than the rest of its category.

3

Names it from the words that set it apart, and flags it as a burning issue.

From a problem statement to an owner

Issue detail

  • A problem statement: who is affected, what happens, and the impact, instead of a one-word label.
  • The exact issue found: the phrases customers actually used, where it happens, and whether it is growing week on week.
  • The root cause written as something to check, never as fact.
  • At most three short fixes, ranked by reach, impact and confidence.

Control centre

  • Product managers and the tech team propose solutions and agree an escalation matrix in one shared place.
  • Everything is saved to a Google Sheet, so the team can also edit it there.
  • Roles with passwords: viewers read, the tech team moves work forward, product managers approve.

It keeps reminding until it is fixed

  • Reminders every 30 minutes in the app, naming the top open problems, so they stay in view all day.
  • Resolved can be updated by the team in the Control centre or straight in the Google Sheet, and resolved problems drop out of the reminders.
  • Multilingual: reviews in 8 languages and Hinglish emails are read as written.

Resolved means the complaints stopped

Marking a fix as resolved is a claim. The tool checks that claim against what customers say next.

  • For every resolved solution it compares complaints before and after the date it was resolved.
  • If three or more complaints arrive afterwards, the problem is tagged Resolved but still happening, goes back to the top of the burning issues and returns to the reminders.
  • Everyone sees the same status on Overview, so the loop is closed in public.
